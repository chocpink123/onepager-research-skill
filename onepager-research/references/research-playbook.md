# Research Playbook

Use this reference when the request needs current market numbers, app activity, social-platform data, or a defensible competitor comparison.

## Evidence capture

For each claim, record:

```text
Claim:
Geography:
Category / definition:
Period:
Metric and unit:
Source:
URL:
Confidence: high / medium / low
```

Do not write a number into the one-pager until its category and period are clear. “Beauty market,” “skincare market,” “retail sales,” “GMV,” “downloads,” “users,” “reach,” “CPM,” and “CAC” must remain distinct.

## Source hierarchy

1. Official company, product, API, or regulatory source for product definitions and technical capabilities.
2. Government statistics, industry associations, platform reports, and audited company reports for market and category data.
3. Established research firms and app-intelligence providers for market estimates, downloads, rankings, and category activity.
4. Reputable journalism only when primary data is unavailable, with the limitation stated.

Do not use search-result snippets as final evidence. Open the source and verify the exact wording, date, geography, and unit.

## App and AI-skincare data

Public reports often group skincare apps inside broader categories such as Fashion & Beauty, Health & Fitness, or Shopping. State the category scope explicitly. Use app downloads, monthly active users, rankings, review counts, and retention only when the source defines the metric. Do not claim a standalone “AI skincare app market size” unless a source defines that market separately.

Useful research queries:

- `2025 fashion beauty app downloads AppTweak`
- `2025 skincare app downloads users skin analysis app`
- `country Digital 2025 Instagram TikTok users`
- `country beauty personal care market 2025 skincare`
- `country trade association beauty personal care ranking`

## Market comparison discipline

Every country row should use comparable columns. A compact executive table can use:

- population and GDP per capita;
- Instagram and TikTok audience, if available from the same report family;
- skincare market size and demand drivers;
- creator fee or CPM, clearly labeled as a media benchmark rather than CAC;
- competitor coverage and Lumi's product-validation value.

When one country has a metric that another does not, either find the same metric for both or remove it from the comparison. Put nuanced conclusions in “product validation value,” not in a separate analysis column, unless the user explicitly requests a decision column.

## Competitor evidence

Capture four things per competitor:

1. Exact official product name and positioning.
2. Observable core functions from the official listing or product page.
3. Activity evidence such as downloads, ratings, reviews, or ranking, with platform and date.
4. The narrow gap that the proposed product can enter.

Preserve screenshots supplied by the user. If a screenshot is local, embed it into the final HTML; never leave a relative `assets/` reference when portability is required.

## Writing the conclusion

Use a three-part sentence:

```text
Evidence → implication → product decision
```

Example:

```text
High skincare-content activity and large social-commerce reach → users discover and discuss products in-feed → validate AI skin analysis, product recognition, and Buddy follow-up in Indonesia first.
```

Do not overstate causality. A low CPM does not prove low CAC; high downloads do not prove retention; a large skincare market does not prove willingness to pay for a new app.
